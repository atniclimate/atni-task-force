The five resolutions that ground the task forces, then the public sources most useful for getting started in each area.

Grounding resolutions

  1. Resolution #2026-034: Endorsement of the Tiered Sovereign Data Framework (TSDF) as an ATNI-Recognized Standard for Indigenous Data Sovereignty (PDF)

    Resolution, Affiliated Tribes of Northwest Indians, 09/17/2026Tribal-specific

    Adopted at the 2026 Annual Convention in Snoqualmie, Washington. Endorses the Tiered Sovereign Data Framework as an ATNI-recognized standard for Indigenous Data Sovereignty, authorizes its release as version 1.0 under a Creative Commons noncommercial share-alike license, and affirms its four tiers: T0 Open/Public, T1 Community Network, T2 Negotiated Partner, and T3 Sovereign Restricted. The endorsement obligates no Tribe to adopt the framework; each Tribe decides its own use.

  2. Resolution #2026-039: The ATNI Energy Committee Supports the Formation of a Task Force on Artificial Intelligence (AI) (PDF)

    Resolution, Affiliated Tribes of Northwest Indians, 09/17/2026Tribal-specific

    Adopted at the 2026 Annual Convention in Snoqualmie, Washington. Calls for a task force, operating under the ATNI Energy Committee or added to an existing ATNI task force such as data centers, to identify the common interests of member Tribes in artificial intelligence technologies, laws, and policies, propose a plan for outreach and education, and support Tribal consultation with federal, state, and local governments and AI companies.

  3. Resolution #2026-051: Supporting Responsible Governance of Youth Use of Artificial Intelligence Chatbots in Schools and Education (PDF)

    Resolution, Affiliated Tribes of Northwest Indians, 09/17/2026Tribal-specific

    Adopted at the 2026 Annual Convention in Snoqualmie, Washington, carrying concerns raised by the ATNI Youth Committee. Urges Tribal and public schools to set strong ethical safeguards for artificial intelligence in education, supports policies that keep it a supplement to critical thinking, cultural education, language revitalization, Elders, and human relationships rather than a replacement, and urges state and federal governments to consult with Tribes on protections for American Indian and Alaska Native youth.

  4. Resolution #2024-35: Calling for Tribal Leadership and Oversight in Electric Grid Governance and Electric Transmission Planning (PDF)

    Resolution, Affiliated Tribes of Northwest Indians, 10/03/2024Tribal-specific

    Adopted at the 2024 Annual Convention in Ridgefield, Washington. Calls on states, regulators, utilities, and other decision makers to give Tribal electric utilities, Tribal energy development organizations, and Tribal elected leaders oversight and leadership roles in forming and governing a Western Regional Transmission Organization, calls for free, prior, and informed consent in transmission planning at every level, and asks the National Congress of American Indians to establish a national Tribal working group on these issues.

  5. Resolution #2024-23: Establishing an ATNI Task Force on Offshore Wind Projects (PDF)

    Resolution, Affiliated Tribes of Northwest Indians, 05/23/2024Tribal-specific

    Adopted at the 2024 Midyear Convention in Canyonville, Oregon. Establishes a limited-duration task force to research offshore wind, propose policy to the ATNI membership, align advocacy, and support member Tribes in their own advocacy. Records concerns about impacts on Tribal culture, sovereignty, sacred sites, submerged cultural resources, water, fish, and migratory species, and authorizes the Executive Board to press for a halt, meaningful consultation, and funding for Tribal engagement.

Tribal Energy

  1. Makah Tribe Strategic Energy Plan (PNNL-38051) (PDF)

    Report, Pacific Northwest National Laboratory, 2025Tribal-specific

    ETIPP plan with outage data, solar and wind potential, wave and biomass assessments, and resilience hub priorities for a remote Northwest coastal Tribe.

  2. Tribal Energy Vision (2022 Energy Vision)

    Policy, Columbia River Inter-Tribal Fish Commission, 2022Tribal-specific

    2022 Energy Vision with 43 recommendations for the Columbia Basin and Northwest. Recommendation 31 calls for a siting plan for renewable resources and transmission lines with state, federal and regional partners.

Data Centers

  1. Data Centers and Water: Tribal Considerations (PDF)

    Report, Columbia River Inter-Tribal Fish Commission (posted by Oregon Department of Energy), 03/27/2026Tribal-specific

    CRITFC slide deck on treaty fisheries, instream flows, aquifers, discharges and a transparency checklist for data center siting in the Columbia Basin. Slides carry a March 20, 2025 date, while the file name is dated 03/27/2026.

  2. Data Centers for Tribal Economic Development: Frequently Asked Questions

    Report, U.S. Department of Energy, Office of Indian Energy, 2026Tribal-specific

    Plain-language FAQ on land needs, HEARTH Act leasing, selling power by PPA, water use, and permanent versus construction jobs for Tribes considering data centers.

  3. Yakama Nation Data Center Findings and Recommendations (PDF)

    Report, Confederated Tribes and Bands of the Yakama Nation, 11/10/2025Tribal-specific

    The Yakama Nation's findings to Washington's Data Center Workgroup. They warn of 3,400-6,000 MW of added peak load under conventional cooling and of suspended salmon spill during emergencies, and note that several Tribes are evaluating data centers on Tribal lands.

AI

  1. NCAI Response to Request for Information on the AI Action Plan (letter, 15 March 2025) (PDF)

    Policy, National Congress of American Indians, 03/15/2025Tribal-specific

    NCAI comment asserting that Tribal Nations hold inherent sovereign rights to set standards for AI data use, collection and ownership, based on resolution NC-24-008. It addresses federal AI policy rather than schools.

  2. Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile (NIST AI 600-1)

    Policy, U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology, 2024

    NIST's companion profile to the AI Risk Management Framework for generative AI, covering 12 risk areas such as confabulation, data privacy and human-AI configuration. The final was published in July 2024; the risk list was checked against the April 2024 draft.

Across the task forces

  1. Tiered Sovereign Data Framework (TSDF)

    Policy, Affiliated Tribes of Northwest Indians, 09/16/2026Tribal-specific

    The public repository of the Tiered Sovereign Data Framework, a standard that turns Indigenous Data Sovereignty principles into four data tiers with rules for sharing data, working with outside partners, and using artificial intelligence. It holds the standard, a tier decision guide, and supporting literature reviews under a Creative Commons noncommercial share-alike license. ATNI endorsed the framework in Resolution #2026-034.

  2. Montana Artificial Intelligence in K-12 Education Guidelines, Version 2.0 (PDF)

    Policy, Montana Office of Public Instruction with Montana Digital Academy, 2026

    Advisory state guidance reviewed twice a year. Lists benefits and risks, including bias, inaccuracy, overreliance and substitution for human instruction. Says better immediate task performance does not by itself show mastery, retention or transfer.

  3. The CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance

    Academic research, Data Science Journal (Carroll et al.), 2020Tribal-specific

    Defines Collective Benefit, Authority to Control, Responsibility and Ethics for Indigenous data. Holds that Indigenous Peoples must determine data governance protocols, a baseline for school and language-tool data agreements.
