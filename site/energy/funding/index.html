What a Tribe can apply for now, and by when

Most federal support for Tribal energy moves by formula, rebate, loan, or tax credit, so “open” means a program is operating, not that money is waiting. Each status was read on the agency’s own page; confirm it there before relying on it.

Programs that fit a Tribe’s own plans

A match pairs an energy type from the Tribe’s own public plans with programs that name that type or may fit it. It does not say the Tribe is eligible, would be competitive, or should apply; the Tribe decides. The types come from What Tribal Plans Say, where each Tribe can ask for a correction.

Bonneville Power Administration

BPA Tribal efficiency grants

BPA Tribal Income Qualified Energy Conservation Grants

Open Status checked 11/05/2026 on bpa.gov

Deadline 11/05/2026: Application due date to ensure funding is available; later applications accepted while funds last.

'Grant applications are now being accepted for fiscal year 2027.' Priority application due date 10/01/2026; 'November 5, 2026 and Beyond: Application due date to ensure funding is available, but applications will be accepted throughout the year as funding is available.'

How it pays
non-competitive grant to Tribal governments
Tribal eligibility
Tribal governments across BPA's service territory; households must be income qualified (Tribes may follow general guidelines or set their own with BPA approval).
Funding level
About $600,000 per year, negotiated annually.
Where it applies
BPA service territory (Pacific Northwest); homes must be served by a BPA customer utility
Energy types named
Energy efficiency and weatherization
May fit; confirm with the agency
Electrification (heat pumps and electric vehicles)

What the program pays for

  • BPA: 'BPA offers income qualified (IQ) energy conservation direct and non-competitive grants to Tribal governments. Tribal IQ grants fund the same energy efficiency technologies as the state grant program, but funds are allocated directly to the Tribe in a government-to-government partnership.'
  • 'Funding for the Tribal IQ grant program budget is approximately $600,000 per year and is negotiated annually.' Homes must be occupied by a member of a federally recognized Tribe, receive electricity from a BPA customer utility, and have electric heat as the primary heat source for most measures.

Cooling in extreme heat

  • Weatherization (insulation, air sealing, shading): may fit; confirm with the agency. Energy conservation upgrades for Tribal member homes with electric heat are funded; the measure list is in BPA's grant scope of work, not on the page read.
  • Heat pumps (heating and cooling): may fit; confirm with the agency. Efficient heating and cooling equipment may be among the funded measures; heat pumps are not named on the page read.

The page says the grants fund the same energy efficiency technologies as the state program without listing them.

U.S. Department of Energy, Office of Indian Energy Policy and Programs

DOE Indian Energy technical assistance

DOE Office of Indian Energy Technical Assistance for Tribal Energy Projects

Open Status checked 10/29/2026 on energy.gov

Listed as 'Ongoing' on the Office's current opportunities page. Each request is evaluated on its scope and the available budget. This is technical help, not a grant.

How it pays
technical assistance at no cost
Tribal eligibility
Federally recognized Indian Tribes and Tribal entities, including Alaska Native regional corporations and village corporations; the page lists the Tribal entities eligible to request assistance.
Energy types named
Energy efficiency and weatherization, Microgrids, Electrification (heat pumps and electric vehicles)
May fit; confirm with the agency
Solar energy, Wind energy, Hydropower, Geothermal energy, Biomass energy, Battery and energy storage

What the program pays for

  • 'Technical assistance for Tribal energy projects is provided at no-cost by experts from the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) Office of Indian Energy, DOE's national laboratories, and other partnering organizations.'
  • It 'assists federally recognized Indian Tribes and Tribal entities, including Alaska Native regional corporations and village corporations, with energy planning, energy efficiency assessments, resource assessments, project planning, energy codes and utility formation activities.'
  • 'Energy resilience planning helps determine critical loads for the Tribe in addition to energy project sizing to provide energy resilience.' 'Modeling and analysis technical assistance can include microgrid modeling and assessment, energy resource generation modeling for a specific energy project, and building modeling for energy use and savings.'
  • Building efficiency assistance 'may include an assessment of heat use in a Tribal building or community and/or a prefeasibility study of other heating options such as waste heat recovery, air source heat pumps, and thermal electric storage systems.' The Office 'cannot advise or facilitate a grant application.'

Cooling in extreme heat

  • Heat pumps (heating and cooling): named by a program document. Building efficiency assistance may include a prefeasibility study of air source heat pumps (a study by experts, not equipment funding).
  • Microgrids: named by a program document. Modeling and analysis assistance can include microgrid modeling and assessment (a study by experts, not construction funding).
  • Backup power for cooling and critical loads: may fit; confirm with the agency. Energy resilience planning determines critical loads and sizes energy projects for resilience; backup power is not named.
  • Weatherization (insulation, air sealing, shading): may fit; confirm with the agency. Building efficiency assessments identify efficiency options for Tribal buildings and homes; weatherization work itself is not funded.

Resource assessments and energy options analysis are described without naming technologies, so generation types are listed as possible.

U.S. Department of Energy, Office of Energy Dominance Financing (EDF, formerly the Loan Programs Office)

DOE Tribal Energy Financing (loans)

Tribal Energy Financing Program (TEFP), formerly the Tribal Energy Loan Guarantee Program (TELGP)

Open Status checked 11/05/2026 on energy.gov

Deadline 08/31/2028: DOE's last submission date, not a competitive round deadline.

EDF's pre-application page offers 'Request a Pre-Application Consultation for the TEFP Program.' CRS reports the solicitation takes submissions until 08/31/2028, that Public Law 119-21 section 50402 rescinded unobligated Inflation Reduction Act funding and left $10.5 million for the loan subsidy, and that one loan guarantee has been issued ($72.8 million, Viejas Microgrid project) since the first solicitation in 2018.

How it pays
loan guarantee and direct loan
Tribal eligibility
An Indian Tribe or a Tribal energy development organization (CRS, citing section 503 of the Energy Policy Act of 2005); the EDF plan describes projects owned and operated by Tribal entities.
Funding level
Loan authority up to $20 billion (EDF strategic plan). Loan subsidy funding of $10.5 million remains after the rescission, and $6.3 million was appropriated for administrative expenses in FY2026 (CRS, 05/22/2026).
Energy types named
Solar energy, Wind energy, Hydropower, Geothermal energy, Biomass energy, Battery and energy storage, Microgrids
May fit; confirm with the agency
Biogas and biofuels, Hydrogen and fuel cells, Energy efficiency and weatherization, Electrification (heat pumps and electric vehicles)

What the program pays for

  • EDF strategic plan: the Tribal Energy Financing Program (Energy Policy Act of 1992, Section 2602(c)) 'Provides loans or loan guarantees up to $20 billion for energy development projects on Tribal lands.' It supports Tribal energy sovereignty 'through financing of conventional and renewable energy projects, transmission infrastructure, and energy storage systems owned and operated by Tribal entities.'
  • EDF home page: 'EDF offers financing for: ... Tribal investment in energy-related projects.'
  • CRS (05/22/2026): section 503 of the Energy Policy Act of 2005 'authorized DOE to guarantee up to 90% of a loan (including principal and interest) made to an Indian Tribe or a Tribal energy development organization for energy development.' Guaranteed loans must include a 'subsidy cost' payment, which 'can be paid through appropriations, by the applicant, or a combination thereof.'

Cooling in extreme heat

  • Microgrids: named by a program document. TEFP finances energy projects and storage owned by Tribal entities, and the one loan guarantee issued so far ($72.8 million) was for the Viejas Microgrid project.
  • Backup power for cooling and critical loads: may fit; confirm with the agency. Energy storage systems owned and operated by Tribal entities can be financed; battery backup for cooling or critical loads is not named.

The EDF plan says 'conventional and renewable energy projects, transmission infrastructure, and energy storage systems'; individual renewable technologies are not listed, so solar, wind, hydropower, geothermal and biomass are read from 'renewable energy projects'. The one guarantee issued financed a microgrid. Confirm current EDF policy on wind and solar with the agency.

Assistance listing: Not shown on the pages checked.

U.S. Department of Homeland Security, Federal Emergency Management Agency

FEMA HMGP

FEMA Hazard Mitigation Grant Program (HMGP)

Open after a disaster declaration Status checked 10/29/2026 on fema.gov

Conditional: funding becomes available only after a Presidentially declared disaster. FEMA's page directs prospective applicants to their State, Tribe or territory hazard mitigation office. The TCR registry reports that FEMA's extreme-heat guidance pages returned 'This file is unpublished' on 10/29/2026; that was not re-checked here.

How it pays
post-disaster grant
Tribal eligibility
FEMA: state, local, Tribal and territorial governments, each with an adopted hazard mitigation plan. HMA Guide B.4 (as quoted in the TCR registry): 'A federally recognized Tribal government has the option to apply for the HMA programs through the state as a subapplicant (when permitted) or directly to FEMA as an applicant.'
Assistance listing
97.039
Energy types named
Solar energy, Battery and energy storage, Microgrids

What the program pays for

  • FEMA: HMGP 'provides funding to state, local, Tribal and territorial governments to develop hazard mitigation plans and rebuild in ways that reduce or mitigate future disaster losses in their communities. This funding becomes available following a Presidentially declared disaster.' Eligible projects include 'Retrofitting buildings to make them more resistant to floods, earthquakes, wind, wildfires and other natural hazards' and 'Upgrading utilities and infrastructure to enhance resistance to natural hazards.'
  • FEMA Hazard Mitigation Assistance Program and Policy Guide (March 23, 2023), as quoted in the TCR registry: D.2.11, 'The purchase and installation of secondary power sources and related equipment, such as generators, microgrids, solar photovoltaic systems, and battery back-up systems are generally eligible if they are cost-effective'; B.11.2.3, 'Microgrids are eligible under HMGP, HMGP Post Fire and BRIC. Unlike generators or solar photovoltaic secondary power sources, microgrids are not limited to critical facilities.' Part 12 lists, for extreme heat, nature-based solutions such as green roofs and increased tree canopy.

Cooling in extreme heat

  • Backup power for cooling and critical loads: named by a program document. Pays for secondary power sources and related equipment, such as generators, solar photovoltaic systems and battery back-up systems, if cost-effective (HMA Guide D.2.11, quoted in the TCR registry); generators and solar are limited to critical facilities.
  • Microgrids: named by a program document. Microgrids are eligible under HMGP and, unlike generators or solar, are not limited to critical facilities (HMA Guide B.11.2.3, quoted in the TCR registry).
  • Cool roofs and reflective surfaces: may fit; confirm with the agency. The Guide names green roofs and tree canopy for extreme heat and climate-smart building materials that 'reduce or help withstand extreme temperatures'; cool roofs are not named.
  • Cooling centers and heat refuges: may fit; confirm with the agency. Retrofitting a public building or backing it up with secondary power could serve a cooling center; the TCR registry reports FEMA's cooling center guidance was unpublished, and cooling centers are not named on the program page.
  • Air conditioning: may fit; confirm with the agency. Retrofits for extreme temperatures are described, but cooling systems for public buildings are not named in the sources re-read.

Solar photovoltaic systems and battery back-up systems are eligible as secondary power sources; generators and solar are limited to critical facilities, microgrids are not.

Assistance listing: As recorded from the FEMA Hazard Mitigation Assistance Guide in the TCR registry; Grants.gov lists no notices under this number.

U.S. Department of Energy, Office of Electricity (with the National Energy Technology Laboratory)

Grid Resilience formula grants (IIJA 40101(d))

Grid Resilience State and Tribal Formula Grants (Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act section 40101(d))

Open Status checked 11/05/2026 on energy.gov

Deadline 02/17/2027: FY25-FY26 application deadline, 11:59 pm EST.

DOE issued Amendment 10 of the ALRD on 10/20/2026, opening a 120-day application period for FY25-FY26 funds (Years 4 and 5 of the 5-year program) for existing grant recipients and new applicants.

How it pays
formula grant to Tribes
Tribal eligibility
Federally recognized Indian Tribes, including Alaska Native Regional Corporations and Alaska Native Village Corporations; each Tribe submits an application with a plan adopted after notice and a public hearing (42 U.S.C. 18711(d)).
Funding level
FY25 and FY26 allocations combined total $967,104,372 across all eligible applicants; DOE's allocation table lists each Tribe's amount.
Energy types named
Battery and energy storage, Microgrids
May fit; confirm with the agency
Solar energy

What the program pays for

  • DOE: the program 'is designed to strengthen and modernize America's power grid against wildfires, severe storms, and other natural disasters' and 'will distribute funding to states, territories, and federally recognized Indian Tribes, including Alaska Native Regional Corporations and Alaska Native Village Corporations, over five years based on a formula.'
  • 42 U.S.C. 18711(e)(1): grants may be used for 'activities, technologies, equipment, and hardening measures to reduce the likelihood and consequences of disruptive events, including ... the use or construction of distributed energy resources for enhancing system adaptive capacity during disruptive events, including (i) microgrids; and (ii) battery-storage subcomponents.' The list also names monitoring and control technologies, undergrounding, and power line hardening. ('Weatherization technologies and equipment' in that list means hardening grid equipment against weather, not home weatherization.)
  • Grants may not be used to build a new electric generating facility, or a large-scale battery storage facility that is not used for enhancing system adaptive capacity during disruptive events (42 U.S.C. 18711(e)(2)). Each Tribe must match 15 percent of its grant.

Cooling in extreme heat

  • Backup power for cooling and critical loads: named by a program document. Pays for battery-storage subcomponents and other distributed energy resources that add adaptive capacity during outages (42 U.S.C. 18711(e)); DOE's page links a guide to installing back-up battery power systems on Tribal facilities.
  • Microgrids: named by a program document. Microgrids are named as an eligible use for enhancing system adaptive capacity during disruptive events (42 U.S.C. 18711(e)(1)).

Solar can be part of a distributed energy resource used for adaptive capacity, but the statute bars new generating facilities as such.

Assistance listing: Not shown on the pages checked.

HUD IHBG

Indian Housing Block Grant (IHBG), formula and competitive (IHBG-COMP)

Open; competitive notice forecasted Status checked 10/29/2026 on hud.gov

The formula grant is distributed every year through each recipient's Indian Housing Plan. The competitive IHBG-COMP notice for FY2026 (PIH-2600-DC-0048) is forecasted on Grants.gov (forecast dated 06/08/2026, about $125 million, up to 30 awards); the FY2025 IHBG-COMP closed 01/15/2026.

How it pays
formula grant, plus a competitive grant program
Tribal eligibility
'Eligible IHBG recipients are federally recognized Tribes, Tribally Designated Housing Entities (TDHEs), and a limited number of State-recognized Tribes.' Each year recipients submit an Indian Housing Plan.
Assistance listing
14.867
Energy types named
Energy efficiency and weatherization
May fit; confirm with the agency
Electrification (heat pumps and electric vehicles), Solar energy

What the program pays for

  • HUD: 'It is a formula grant that is distributed annually and funds affordable housing activities including new housing construction, rehabilitation, and housing services, and much more.'
  • 25 U.S.C. 4132(2), development: 'The acquisition, new construction, reconstruction, or moderate or substantial rehabilitation of affordable housing, which may include real property acquisition, site improvement, development and rehabilitation of utilities, necessary infrastructure, and utility services, conversion, demolition, financing, administration and planning, improvement to achieve greater energy efficiency, mold remediation, and other related activities.'

Cooling in extreme heat

  • Weatherization (insulation, air sealing, shading): named by a program document. IHBG pays for 'improvement to achieve greater energy efficiency' as part of developing, building or rehabilitating affordable housing (25 U.S.C. 4132(2)).
  • Air conditioning: may fit; confirm with the agency. Rehabilitation and new construction of Tribal housing can include cooling equipment; air conditioners are not named in the statute or on HUD's page.
  • Heat pumps (heating and cooling): may fit; confirm with the agency. Heat pump installation would be a rehabilitation or new construction cost; it is not named.

Energy efficiency improvements are named in the statute; heat pumps or solar in new or rehabilitated housing would be housing development costs but are not named.

U.S. Department of the Treasury, Internal Revenue Service

IRS elective pay

Elective pay (direct pay) for clean energy tax credits, Internal Revenue Code section 6417, as changed by Public Law 119-21

Open, with limits Status checked 10/29/2026 on irs.gov

Elective pay remains available to Tribal governments, but Public Law 119-21 changed what it pays for. The July 4, 2026 construction-start date for wind and solar has passed, so a new wind or solar project that has not begun construction must be placed in service by 12/31/2027. The beginning-of-construction rules in Notice 2025-42 may have changed after a court decision reported in June 2026; that was not confirmed on an official page, so confirm current rules with the IRS or a tax advisor.

How it pays
tax credit paid as a refund to the Tribal government
Tribal eligibility
IRS FAQ Q4: 'An Indian Tribal government, subdivision thereof, or any agency or instrumentality of a Tribal government or political subdivision is eligible for elective pay.' The December 2025 final rule (TD 10039, effective 01/15/2026) treats entities wholly owned by Tribes as instrumentalities for elective pay. The entity must register with the IRS before filing.
Funding level
Credit value of 6 percent of qualified investment, or 30 percent with prevailing wage and apprenticeship compliance, with bonuses for energy communities and domestic content (Publication 5817-G; FAQ).
Energy types named
Solar energy, Wind energy, Battery and energy storage
May fit; confirm with the agency
Hydropower, Marine energy (tidal or wave), Geothermal energy, Biomass energy, Biogas and biofuels, Hydrogen and fuel cells, Microgrids

What the program pays for

  • IRS: 'Elective pay makes certain clean energy tax credits ... effectively refundable ... The entity can receive the full value of the credit because the IRS treats the elective payment amount as a tax payment. We then count it as overpayment on the return and refund it to the entity.'
  • IRS Publication 5817-G describes the Clean Electricity Investment Credit (section 48E) as a 'technology-neutral tax credit for investment in facilities that generate clean electricity and qualified energy storage technologies,' at 6 percent of qualified investment or 30 percent if prevailing wage and apprenticeship rules are met. For credits before 2025 it lists section 45 'wind, biomass, geothermal, solar, small irrigation, landfill and trash, hydropower, marine and hydrokinetic energy' and section 48 'fuel cell, solar, geothermal, small wind, energy storage, biogas, microgrid controllers, and combined heat and power.'
  • Public Law 119-21: sections 45Y and 48E end for 'an applicable wind facility or applicable solar facility that is placed in service after December 31, 2027'; the termination applies to wind and solar 'the construction of which begins after July 4, 2026' (IRS Notice 2025-42). The 45W commercial clean vehicle credit 'will not be allowed for any vehicle acquired after Sept. 30, 2025' (IRS).

Cooling in extreme heat

  • Backup power for cooling and critical loads: may fit; confirm with the agency. Energy storage technology qualifies for the section 48E investment credit, so a battery system that backs up cooling loads can earn the credit if it meets the credit's rules; backup power is not named.
  • Microgrids: may fit; confirm with the agency. Publication 5817-G lists microgrid controllers as section 48 property for pre-2025 construction; whether a microgrid's components qualify under 48E depends on the components.

Section 48E names qualified facilities and energy storage technology and ends for wind and solar placed in service after 2027. Publication 5817-G names the other technologies only for the pre-2025 credits (sections 45 and 48) and calls sections 45Y and 48E technology-neutral, so they are listed as possible; confirm a facility's eligibility with a tax advisor.

Assistance listing: A tax provision, not a grant program.

U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, Administration for Children and Families, Office of Community Services

LIHEAP

Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP), including Tribal grantees

Open Status checked 10/29/2026 on liheapch.acf.gov

On 10/09/2026 ACF announced the first FY2027 funding release: about $3.6 billion of regular block grant funds released to all LIHEAP grantees, equal to 90 percent of funds under the continuing resolution (Public Law 119-103, signed 09/02/2026), allocated by the annual formula update. Grantees need an approved FY2027 Model Plan to draw funds.

How it pays
block grant (formula) to states, Tribes and territories
Tribal eligibility
Indian Tribes and Tribal organizations receive LIHEAP grants directly when the Secretary determines that Tribal members would be better served by direct grants (42 U.S.C. 8623(d)); ACF publishes allocation tables for Tribal grant recipients. Assistance goes to low-income households through the Tribal LIHEAP office; LIHEAP does not give direct grants to individuals.
Funding level
About $3.6 billion of FY2027 regular block grant funds released to all grantees on 10/09/2026; Tribal amounts are in ACF's allocation tables.
Assistance listing
93.568
Energy types named
Energy efficiency and weatherization
May fit; confirm with the agency
Electrification (heat pumps and electric vehicles)

What the program pays for

  • ACF: 'LIHEAP provides federally funded assistance to reduce the costs associated with home energy bills, energy crises, weatherization, and minor energy-related home repairs. LIHEAP can help you stay warm in the winter and cool in the summer through programs that reduce the risk of health and safety problems that arise from unsafe heating and cooling situations and practices.'
  • 42 U.S.C. 8622(6): 'The term home energy means a source of heating or cooling in residential dwellings.' Grantees assist low-income households with home energy costs, 'intervene in energy crisis situations' and 'provide low-cost residential weatherization and other cost-effective energy-related home repair' (42 U.S.C. 8624(b)(1)).
  • The LIHEAP Clearinghouse tells Tribal grantees to plan spending for each type of assistance (heating, cooling, crisis or weatherization) and publishes Tribal benefit levels for heating, cooling and crisis.

Cooling in extreme heat

  • Weatherization (insulation, air sealing, shading): named by a program document. LIHEAP can pay for low-cost residential weatherization and other cost-effective energy-related home repair (42 U.S.C. 8624(b)(1)), at the grantee's option within its plan.
  • Air conditioning: may fit; confirm with the agency. LIHEAP covers 'home energy,' defined as heating or cooling, and Tribal plans set cooling benefit levels; whether a given Tribal plan pays for buying or repairing air conditioners or fans varies by plan.

Weatherization and minor energy-related home repair are named; equipment replacement depends on each grantee's plan.

Assistance listing: Not shown on the pages checked; this is the number HHS publishes for LIHEAP. Confirm on SAM.gov.

U.S. Department of Energy, Office of Critical Minerals and Energy Innovation (Home Energy Rebates Program)

Tribal HEEHR home rebates (IRA 50122)

High-Efficiency Electric Home Rebate (HEEHR) Program for Indian Tribes (Inflation Reduction Act section 50122), with its Administrative and Legal Requirements Document (ALRD)

Open Status checked 11/05/2026 on energy.gov

Deadline 12/11/2026: Tribes must apply to DOE for their formula awards by this date (Program Notice 26-3)

Program Notice 26-3 reopened the Tribal application period; the deadline is 12/11/2026. DOE's general Home Energy Rebates page says details on active state, territory or Tribal programs are 'coming soon.' Grantees work with their DOE Project Officer. The ALRD (Version 2, 11/07/2024) and the Program Requirements and Application Instructions (Version 2) remain in effect except where the notice changes them.

How it pays
formula grant to Tribes that pays household rebates
Tribal eligibility
Indian Tribes and Alaska Native Corporations apply to DOE for non-competitive formula awards; Tribes can form a consortium or name a third-party agent such as a Tribally designated housing entity. Rebates go to low- and moderate-income households. Program Notice 26-3 also extends low-income categorical eligibility to housing that receives Indian Housing Block Grant funding.
Funding level
$225 million appropriated for Indian Tribes (42 U.S.C. 18795a); about $218.25 million in formula allocations after the 3 percent administrative and technical assistance set-aside (DOE fact sheet).
Energy types named
Electrification (heat pumps and electric vehicles), Energy efficiency and weatherization

What the program pays for

  • 42 U.S.C. 18795a(a)(1)(B): 'to award grants to Indian Tribes to develop and implement a high-efficiency electric home rebate program ... $225,000,000, to remain available through September 30, 2031.'
  • Rebates cover 'not more than $8,000 for a heat pump for space heating or cooling' (42 U.S.C. 18795a(c)(3)). A qualified project includes 'an electric heat pump for space heating and cooling,' insulation, 'air sealing and materials to improve ventilation,' and electric wiring or a load service center.
  • DOE fact sheet (September 2024): $218.25 million 'in non-competitive formula allocations to Indian Tribes and Alaska Native Corporations'; rebates can cover up to full project cost for low-income households (below 80% of area median income) and up to half for moderate-income households (80 to 150%); an electric heat pump HVAC system is capped at $8,000, with up to $14,000 per dwelling unit in total.
  • Program Notice 26-3 (effective 05/29/2026): DOE will 'reopen and extend the application deadline'; 'Tribes must apply for their formula awards from the Department by December 11, 2026.' It allows 'rebates for upgrading HVAC and appliances only from existing electric equipment to more efficient electric equipment,' keeps HVAC and appliances in new construction allowable, requires insulation and air sealing before heating and cooling upgrades unless a home is already sealed to a DOE-approved, Tribally specified level, and allows 'dwellings to retain existing fossil-fuel HVAC systems when installing a heat pump.'

Cooling in extreme heat

  • Heat pumps (heating and cooling): named by a program document. HEEHR section 50122 rebates cover electric heat pumps for space heating and cooling (up to $8,000 per household in the statute), and Program Notice 26-3 lets a dwelling keep an existing fossil-fuel system when installing a heat pump.
  • Weatherization (insulation, air sealing, shading): named by a program document. Rebates cover insulation, air sealing and ventilation, and Program Notice 26-3 requires them before heating and cooling upgrades unless the home is already sealed to a DOE-approved, Tribally specified level.
  • Air conditioning: may fit; confirm with the agency. Program Notice 26-3 allows HVAC upgrades from existing electric equipment to more efficient electric equipment; cooling-only air conditioners are not named in the sources checked.

Assistance listing: Not shown on the pages checked.
