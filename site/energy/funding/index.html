Tribal Energy Funding

Most federal support for Tribal energy does not arrive as a competitive grant with a deadline. It moves by formula allocation, household rebate, loan guarantee, or tax credit, and the agency pages that govern it change within a season.

Read This First

This page tracks federal programs and the Bonneville Power Administration’s Tribal efficiency grant, with each status read from the agency’s own page, the Grants.gov record, or the statute on a stated date, and pairs them with the energy types each Tribe’s own public plans name (What Tribal Plans Say; method).

Three rules travel with every entry. A program’s presence for an energy type is a program fact, not a finding that a Tribe is eligible, would be competitive, or should apply. “Open” for a formula, rebate, loan, or tax program means the program is operating, not that money is waiting. Statuses are as of the date shown and should be confirmed with the agency before a council relies on one. Tax questions, including how Public Law 119-21 affects elective pay for a Tribally owned project, are for each Tribe’s counsel and tax advisers.

State, utility, and philanthropic programs are not yet in the catalog. Check the Tribe’s state energy office and electric utility alongside this list.

Caveats That Travel With the Data

These are program facts and screening flags, not eligibility determinations or recommendations.
  • These are program facts and screening flags, not eligibility determinations or recommendations. The Tribe decides whether and how to apply.
  • Program statuses change quickly. Each was read on the date shown on the program’s page; confirm with the agency before relying on it.
  • A match pairs an energy type, ranked from the Tribe’s own public planning documents, with programs that name that type or may fit it. It does not say the Tribe is eligible, would be competitive, or should apply.
  • A type marked “may fit, confirm with the agency” is not named in the program’s documents.
  • A forecasted opportunity has no close date; the agency’s estimated dates are not deadlines. Opportunity listings come from Grants.gov and state who the agency says may apply.
  • The ranked energy types are a machine-assisted reading of public planning documents and may be incomplete or out of date. Each Tribe is the authority on its own plans.

    Program Catalog

    A program lists an energy type when a program document names it. A type that may fit from a broader eligible category is marked “may fit; confirm with the agency.” Cooling uses follow the same rule.

    What the statuses mean

      Grants.gov Notices

      Grants.gov was searched for posted and forecasted notices open to Tribal governments, in 69 searches by energy type and cooling use, retrieved 10/29/2026 at 1:28 PM PDT. It kept 3 notices: 0 posted and 3 forecasted.

      The absence of a notice is not evidence that funding does not exist. Most Tribal energy money moves by formula, rebate, loan, or tax credit, which Grants.gov does not list, and a program may sit in a state plan or a notice not yet posted.

        This product uses the Grants.gov API but is not endorsed or certified by the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services.
