Data Files

  • energy-funding.json, the program catalog, and its method note (GitHub)

The catalog, the Grants.gov layer, and the per-Tribe matches are open as energy-funding.json. Each program record names its sources and the page that supports each energy type and cooling use.

How the Catalog Is Kept

Most federal support for Tribal energy does not arrive as a competitive grant with a deadline. It moves by formula allocation, household rebate, loan guarantee, or tax credit, and the agency pages that govern it change within a season.

The catalog tracks federal programs and the Bonneville Power Administration’s Tribal efficiency grant, with each status read from the agency’s own page, the Grants.gov record, or the statute on a stated date, and pairs them with the energy types each Tribe’s own public plans name (What Tribal Plans Say). The method note explains how the catalog was checked and how to refresh it. State, utility, and philanthropic programs are not yet in the catalog; check the Tribe’s state energy office and electric utility alongside it.

Three rules travel with every entry. A program’s presence for an energy type is a program fact, not a finding that a Tribe is eligible, would be competitive, or should apply. “Open” for a formula, rebate, loan, or tax program means the program is operating, not that money is waiting. Statuses are as of the date shown and should be confirmed with the agency before a council relies on one. Tax questions, including how Public Law 119-21 affects elective pay for a Tribally owned project, are for each Tribe’s counsel and tax advisers.

  • These are program facts and screening flags, not eligibility determinations or recommendations. The Tribe decides whether and how to apply.
  • A match pairs an energy type, ranked from the Tribe’s own public planning documents, with programs that name that type or may fit it. A type marked “may fit; confirm with the agency” is not named in the program’s documents.
  • A forecasted opportunity has no close date; the agency’s estimated dates are not deadlines.
  • Cooling in extreme heat lists what a program document says about air conditioning, heat pumps, cooling centers, backup power and weatherization, since heat waves are an energy cost and a health risk.
  • The ranked energy types are a machine-assisted reading of public planning documents and may be incomplete or out of date. Each Tribe is the authority on its own plans.

How Grants.gov Was Searched

Grants.gov was searched for posted and forecasted notices open to Tribal governments, by energy type, by cooling use, and by each catalog program’s assistance listing; the counts of each kind are in the record of that retrieval, in energy-funding.json. A notice was kept when its title or synopsis matched an energy keyword or it belonged to a catalog program. The absence of a notice is not evidence that funding does not exist: most Tribal energy money moves by formula, rebate, loan, or tax credit, which Grants.gov does not list, and a program may sit in a state plan or a notice not yet posted.
