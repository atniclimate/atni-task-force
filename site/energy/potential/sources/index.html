    ATNI Energy Task Forces, published 10/29/2026. On Efficiency, solar, and electrification lead 44 Tribes' public plans; no finding concerns offshore wind or marine energy.

Data Files

  • energy-potential.csv and energy-potential.json, the public dataset, and its method note (GitHub)

The CSV file has one row per finding, with its stage, check, and first cited document, year, page, link, and excerpt; each Tribe with no finding has one row with the reason. The JSON file adds every citation, the method, the caveats, and the documents read for each Tribe. Carry the caveats with any use of the data.

How It Was Made, and Its Limits

This is a machine-assisted reading of public plans. It is not a Tribe’s own assessment, it endorses no project, and it ranks no Tribe against another. Each Tribe is the authority on its own plans.

Tribal Nations have written their energy intentions into their own public plans: climate action plans, hazard mitigation plans, economic development strategies, housing plans, and strategic energy plans. The page gathers what those documents say, Tribe by Tribe, for the Tribes listed in ATNI’s directory, so that regional conversations about energy start from what Tribes have already said, in their own documents, rather than from what others assume about them.

How it was made

For each listed Tribe, the public planning documents in ATNI’s plan library were searched page by page for each energy type in the method, from solar, wind, hydropower, geothermal, and biomass to battery storage, microgrids, efficiency, and electrification. Passages around each match were read by a typed-judgment model (TypeSafe Jev), which answered three questions: does the passage describe the Tribe’s own energy work, how far has that work progressed, and was the option found unsuitable. Code ranked the types by the most advanced stage found, a second pass checked each ranked finding against its cited excerpts, and a sample of findings was read by hand against their cited pages. The full method, with every threshold and the checks that changed the design, is in the method note.

How to read it

Each Tribe’s entry lists up to five energy types, each with a stage (installed or operating, planned or funded, assessed or sited, or goal or interest), the documents that support it, and short verbatim excerpts with page links. Open the cited page before relying on a finding. A stage describes what the document reports, not what exists today.

  • Verified: the second pass found that the cited excerpts show the stage claimed.
  • Needs review: the second pass was uncertain. Read the cited page; the stage may be overstated.

What it is not, and its limits

Fossil fuel backup and transmission are not ranked. Offshore wind and marine energy appear in the search but had no ranked finding at the 10/29/2026 build; that reflects the documents in the library, not Tribal positions.

The library holds what public discovery found and could download, so a Tribe’s newest plan may be missing, and some documents still await text extraction. Attribution of a shared plan to one Tribe can be wrong; one finding was withheld for that reason. Old plans are read as written, so the year matters. A Tribe with no finding is in one of three states, each counted on the plate with its own limit. ATNI listing comes from a capture of ATNI’s directory page; it records a listing, not membership certification.

Corrections and Removals

Each Tribe is the authority on its own plans. A Tribe can ask ATNI to correct or remove its entry at any time through ATNI’s contact page. A request can name a single finding (the energy type and the document) or ask that the whole entry come down.
