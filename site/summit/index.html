ATNI Tribal Energy Summit and Working Group

A two-day working session where Tribal leaders, Tribal staff, and partners turn task force findings into draft resolutions, information requests, and statements of need for committees of the Affiliated Tribes of Northwest Indians (ATNI).

A Working Session

The Summit is built for work, not for presentations alone. Day 1 is for learning and asking questions; Day 2 is for drafting. What leaves the room is work product that Tribal leaders can carry into ATNI’s resolution process.

It draws on all three task forces: Tribal Energy Feasibility, Data Centers, and Artificial Intelligence. It is also where the cycle closes. Drafts that find sponsors go to the appropriate ATNI committees, and resolutions adopted by member Tribes set the direction for the next round of task force work.

Agenda

Day 1

Learn and Ask

  1. Plenary: An opening plenary sets out the areas for consideration drawn from the task forces’ work.
  2. World café tables: ATNI partners and projects host tables. Tribal leaders, Tribal staff, and partners rotate among them to learn, ask questions, and leave notes.
  3. Close and homework: An overview of the day, with homework to prepare for drafting on Day 2.

Day 2

Draft and Carry Forward

  1. Synthesis: Facilitators share synthesized notes from the Day 1 tables.
  2. Morning drafting: Tribal leaders draft resolutions, information requests, and statements of identified needs.
  3. Afternoon presentations: Drafts are presented, and sponsors are sought to carry resolutions to the appropriate ATNI committees.

Session times will be posted with the confirmed dates.

Who Hosts the Tables

Tables are hosted by ATNI programs and projects and by agency partners, and hosts will be listed as they confirm.

Tables are hosted by ATNI programs and projects and by the state and federal agency partners ATNI works with. Hosts will be listed here as they confirm.

Two kinds of tables run side by side. ATNI programs and task forces host the tables where Tribal leaders work out what they want to ask for and what they want to say. Agency partners host tables about their own programs and decisions, where Tribal leaders ask what is being decided, when, and how Tribal governments will be asked. A partner host answers and listens; the notes at every table are kept by ATNI.

Each table carries one focus question, drawn from the work of the three task forces, and the public sources behind it are listed in the library before the event. Tables stay at the level of policy. Nothing about a specific place, a specific agreement, or any Tribe’s internal deliberation is recorded; participants are told how notes are handled at the opening and may ask for anything to be struck.

Who Should Attend

  • Tribal leaders: Elected and appointed leaders of ATNI member Tribes, who lead the drafting on Day 2.
  • Tribal staff: Staff who work on energy and utilities, data and technology, education, and the lands and waters these decisions affect.
  • Partners and projects: Organizations and projects working with ATNI, who host world café tables and contribute on Day 1.

Partners host and contribute; Tribal leaders lead the drafting.

How Task Force Work Feeds the Summit

Each task force prepares material for Day 1: the areas for consideration, background research, and questions for the world café tables. The library on this site holds the public sources behind that material.

What Each Task Force Brings

Each task force prepares a plenary framing, a focus question and reading for each table, and a list of open questions.

Each of the three task forces prepares three things for Day 1: a short plenary framing of the questions in its area, the focus question and reading for each of its tables, and a one-page list of the open questions it wants Tribal leaders to weigh.

The Tribal Energy Feasibility Task Force brings Resolution #2024-23 and Resolution #2024-35, what member Tribes’ own public plans say about energy, siting tools that keep a Tribe’s data on its own computers, and the public catalog of funding programs. The Data Center Task Force brings the regional decisions on load, water, and siting that are open in 2027 and the questions a Tribal government can put to any proposal. The AI Task Force brings Resolution #2026-039 and Resolution #2026-051, the common interests of member Tribes in AI law and policy, and what Tribes want to be able to say about AI in their schools. Across all three, the Tiered Sovereign Data Framework endorsed in Resolution #2026-034 sets the terms on which data is shared, including the notes taken in the room.

The work runs in both directions. What Tribal leaders draft on Day 2 becomes each task force’s work plan for the conventions that follow, and the task forces report back to the membership on what was carried, what was answered, and what remains open. Briefings will be listed here as they are prepared.

Registration

Registration details are coming. Dates, venue, and registration will be announced through ATNI. To ask about the Summit or to offer to host a table, contact ATNI through its website.

Dates, Venue, and Registration

Dates and venue are to be confirmed; the Working Group is proposed for March 2027.

Dates and venue are to be confirmed. The Working Group is proposed for March 2027, and ATNI will announce the confirmed dates, the venue, the lodging block, and registration through its website and newsletters. Tribal leaders of member Tribes will receive a direct invitation.

Plan for two full working days. Day 1 runs from an opening in the morning through a late-afternoon close, with table rounds before and after lunch; Day 2 runs from a morning opening through a mid-afternoon close. Registration will ask for each participant’s role (Tribal leader, Tribal staff, Tribal utility or energy organization, partner) and the tables they most want to visit, so that tables can be sized. It will not ask for positions on any topic. Travel information will be posted with the venue.
