Summit

The proposed Tribal Energy Summit

A two-day working session where Tribal leaders, Tribal staff, and partners turn task force findings into draft resolutions, information requests, and statements of need for committees of the Affiliated Tribes of Northwest Indians (ATNI). Day 1 is for learning and asking questions; Day 2 is for drafting.

The Proposed Agenda

Two days, three stops each. Session times will be posted with the confirmed dates. Choose a stop to read it.
What happens
An opening plenary sets out the areas for consideration drawn from the task forces’ work.
Drawn from
Each task force prepares a short plenary framing of the questions in its area.
What happens
ATNI partners and projects host tables. Tribal leaders, Tribal staff, and partners rotate among them to learn, ask questions, and leave notes.
Before you come
Each table carries one focus question, and the public sources behind it are listed in the library before the event.
What is recorded
ATNI keeps the notes at every table, at the level of policy: nothing about a specific place, a specific agreement, or any Tribe’s internal deliberation, and participants may ask for anything to be struck.
What happens
An overview of the day, with homework to prepare for drafting on Day 2.
What happens
Facilitators share synthesized notes from the Day 1 tables.
What happens
Tribal leaders draft resolutions, information requests, and statements of identified needs.
Who leads
Elected and appointed leaders of ATNI member Tribes.
What happens
Drafts are presented, and sponsors are sought to carry resolutions to the appropriate ATNI committees.
After the Summit
Drafts that find sponsors go to the appropriate ATNI committees, and resolutions adopted by member Tribes set the direction for the next round of task force work.

Who Comes, and What to Bring

Tribal leaders

Elected and appointed leaders of ATNI member Tribes, who lead the drafting on Day 2. Each receives a direct invitation.

Before you come

  • Read the resolutions that ground the task forces, in the reading list below.
  • Choose the tables you most want to visit.
  • Come ready to draft on Day 2: a resolution, an information request, or a statement of need.

Tribal staff

Staff who work on energy and utilities, data and technology, education, and the lands and waters these decisions affect.

Before you come

  • Read the public sources for your tables in the library.
  • Bring the questions your Tribe wants the tables to take up.

Partners and table hosts

Organizations and projects working with ATNI, who host world café tables and contribute on Day 1.

Hosts are listed here as they confirm. ATNI programs and task forces host tables where Tribal leaders work out what to ask for; agency partners host tables on their own programs, and answer and listen.

Before you come

  • Prepare one focus question for your table, drawn from the task forces’ work.
  • Send the public sources behind it, so the library can list them before the event.
  • Come to answer and listen; ATNI keeps the notes at every table.

The Reading List

The resolutions behind the task forces, then the library’s Summit material. Each table’s own reading is added to the library before the event. Find more in the library.

Each task force also prepares a short plenary framing of its area, the focus question and reading for each of its tables, and a one-page list of the open questions it wants Tribal leaders to weigh; those briefings will be listed here as they are prepared. What each has gathered so far is on its page: Tribal Energy, Data Centers, and AI.

Ask, Host, or Prepare

To ask about the Summit or to offer to host a table, contact ATNI through its website.

Contact ATNI Build a council briefing

ATNI will announce the confirmed dates, the venue, the lodging block, and registration through its website and newsletters. Plan for two full working days: Day 1 from a morning opening to a late-afternoon close, and Day 2 from a morning opening to a mid-afternoon close. Registration will ask each participant’s role and the tables they most want to visit, so tables can be sized; it will not ask for positions on any topic.
