What should leaders ask a developer?

Eight sets of questions, from consent to exit terms, each led by its first.

  • Consent. Have citizens been asked, in a survey, town hall, or referendum, and what did they say?

  • Land. What is the land status (trust, allotment, fee), and does the Tribe have approved HEARTH Act leasing regulations?

  • Water. What is the cooling design, and how much water does it withdraw and consume across all operating phases?

  • Power. How many megawatts at full load, and who generates it?

  • Rates. Is there a large-load tariff that assigns the facility’s full cost of service, with minimum billing, collateral, and exit charges?

  • Jobs. What are the realistic permanent jobs and wages?

  • Data. If Tribal data will be hosted, under what terms?

  • Exit Terms. What happens if demand for AI computing contracts?

How the Work Moves

Member Tribes have called, by resolution, for Tribal leadership in how energy, data infrastructure, and artificial intelligence (AI) are planned, built, and governed. The task forces carry that direction into practice and answer to ATNI member Tribes.

Four steps, from resolution to draft

Member Tribes adopt resolutions at ATNI conventions. Each task force traces its charge to one or more of them.

The task forces gather public research, track policy, and name the decisions in front of Tribal leaders.

The proposed Tribal Energy Summit and Working Group puts Tribal leaders, Tribal staff, and partners in the same room to hear what the task forces found and ask their own questions.

Tribal leaders draft resolutions, information requests, and statements of need, and seek sponsors to carry them to ATNI committees.

Resolutions adopted by member Tribes start the cycle again.
